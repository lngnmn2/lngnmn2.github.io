That Stupid Pelican On A Bicycle

November 23, 2026 · lngnmn2@yahoo.com

Today I will show you something remarkable.

All the documented and controlled “experiments” which performed controlled comparisons of the LLMs with that degenerate pelican-on-a-bicycle meme “test” produced the result of that fucking pelican facing right – 100%, without a single exception.

Here is a claim, which cannot be imperically verified but can be easily justified from the first principles unudertanding – every major model for the very same prompt (without a single word altered) will produce the image of a pelicane facing right.

Here is how.

The test became a silly meme, but it probably wasn’t out of the blue genuinely random choice – it was a reflection/recollection on somethig which has been read or heard somewhere before.

After the original shitpost, which became a meme, thousands of people tried it just for fun, the slop has been saved by the model operators and feed back into the models as new training examples, reinforcing the “bias”. They are also almost certainly sell your prompt-slop pairs to each other. It is that simple.

Here is a catch.

If you know nothing of the context, (You) could legitimately assume that when pelicans drive a bicycle they always facing right. At least any probabilistic interpretation will produce exactly this conclusion.

But what about the source code? Would something similar happen at the level of the code fragments? Would suboptimal but too common crappy imperative patterns and even these containing a subtle UB (like the fucking C++ “options” which have an Undefined Behavior if you dereference “None”, and the compiler cannot statically check anything, because it is a runtime check – fucking idiots, they never even learn).

One more time. The actual fact that all the images legitimately observed (in a properly controlled experiments) and 99.9% other images are of the damn pelican facing right is an illustration (yes, yes) of the fundamental flaw of the whole technology stack, which is, still, just a glorified autocomplete.

But there is more. This is exactly how socially constructed narratives, stories, religious dogmas and literally everything else has been created and reinforced and maintained within particular societies – the variety is enormous, the underlying mechanics are exactly the same, even within the synaptic gaps – it is just a frequency of exposure without questioning anything.

So, no. No “creativity”, no “reasoning”, no “intelligence”, just probabilistic auto-completion of the most recurrent structural patterns, with pixels or words or code.

Yes, there are fundamental differences between pixels and the code (models draw humans without knowing anything about anatomy, physiology, proportion or gravity – just from the most frequent pixel sequences in vast data files), but the principle is exactly the same – frequencies (real numbers between 0.0 and 1.0) instead of understanding of any kind.

The understanding is just a appearance – a very computationally expensive cognitive illusion, way more sophisticated that the naive meme Turing Test.
